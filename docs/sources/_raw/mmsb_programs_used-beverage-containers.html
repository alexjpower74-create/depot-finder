4 billion containers recycled – and counting!

Since 1997, Green Depots across the province have helped divert beverage cans, bottles, and cartons from landfills.

The Green Depot Network

Green Depots are independently owned and operated enterprises licensed by MMSB to collect used beverage containers from consumers. The operational costs of Green Depots are funded through the beverage container deposits administered by MMSB.

There are currently 52 Green Depots across the province. Green Depots make it convenient for consumers to return their beverage containers for a refund.

How It Works

In our Used Beverage Container Recycling program, a small deposit is applied to most ready-to-drink beverages. Return your empty containers to a Green Depot, and you’ll get a portion of that deposit back. The remaining portion of the deposit helps cover the costs of recycling—like collection, transportation, processing, and administration—keeping the cycle going strong and our environment cleaner.

The following beverage containers are NOT ACCEPTED at your Green Depot:

There is no deposit applied upon purchase to non-program materials.

Milk

As defined under the Canadian Food and Drug Regulations, is deemed an essential nutritional product – no recycling deposit is charged and it is not accepted. This also includes flavoured and goat’s milk

Plant-Based Milk Alternatives

Those that are a source of protein and labelled as ‘fortified’ (such as soy or cashew milk) are not accepted.

TIP: If the container is labelled as ‘not a source of protein’ it is accepted.

Infant Formula

Pre-mixed infant formula containers cannot be recycled at Green Depot.

Meal Replacement Beverages and Formulated Liquid Diets

If the label says ‘Meal Replacement’ or ‘Formulated Liquid Diet’, it is not accepted.

TIP: If the label says ‘Nutritional Supplement’, ‘Shake’, or ‘Protein Shake’, it is accepted.

Concentrated Beverages

Concentrated Beverages (such as frozen juice concentrate) are not accepted at Green Depots.

Distilled Water Containers

Distilled water containers are not accepted at Green Depots.

Containers Greater Than 5 Litres

Containers larger than 5 litres are not accepted at Green Depots.

Beverage Containers Bought Outside of Newfoundland & Labrador

Only beverages purchased in Newfoundland & Labrador can be accepted for refund at Green Depots.

Refillable Containers

Refillable containers that are collected and reused through a local distributor program are not accepted at Green Depots. This includes glass beer bottles from local breweries.

Preparing Your Containers

Beverage containers that are not prepared properly may not be accepted at your Green Depot for a refund. Learn how to get refund ready by following the steps below:

Remove caps, straws, and garbage

Caps, straws, and other debris must be removed before containers are returned to your Green Depot. It matters because:

  • The repeated strain of removing hundreds of caps every day can cause a health and safety issue for staff.
  • During processing, containers are compacted into large cubes called bales. If caps are left on, air build-up inside the containers can cause problems in the baling process.
  • Caps and straws are made of a different type of plastic than containers. Bales containing mixed plastic are lower quality and of lower market value.
  • Containers with garbage, liquid, or debris are considered contaminated and cannot be recycled. Always ensure your containers are empty – especially of any safety hazards such as needles or sharp objects.

Empty all liquid

Empty your containers of all leftover liquids and rinse them out. Containers that aren’t rinsed can cause many issues and may be rejected.

Do not remove labels

Green Depot operators and staff must be able to clearly identify containers to ensure they are included in the Beverage Container Recycling Program. For this reason, labels should be left on your containers.

Do not crush cans

Crushed or flattened containers are difficult to identify and count, causing problems in processing. As more crushed containers would fit in bulk bags, they make those bags too heavy for Green Depot staff to lift safely.

Ensure bottles are not broken

Broken glass bottles are not accepted, as they create a health and safety issue for the people who sort your recyclables.

The Life Cycle of Beverage Containers

Manufacturing

Beverage containers are created in all shapes and sizes and from a range of materials—plastic, aluminum, and glass, for example. These materials can be brand new or, thanks to recycling programs like ours, reclaimed and given a second life.

Once manufactured, the containers are filled, labeled, sealed, packed, and shipped—ready to hit the shelves and be enjoyed again.

Distribution and Sale

Beverages are typically distributed in bulk to retailers, where you’ll find them on store shelves, in vending machines, or chilled in refrigeration units.

When you buy most ready-to-serve beverages in the province, a small deposit is added to your purchase. Part of that deposit helps support the recycling program, while the rest comes back to you when you return the empty container to your local Green Depot for recycling. It’s a simple system that keeps containers out of landfills and supports a cleaner environment.

Consumption and Disposal

Once you’ve finished your drink, give your empty container a second life by returning it to a Green Depot. It’s the easiest way to help the environment and keep recyclable materials out of landfills.

Sorting at the Green Depot

After you return used beverage containers to a Green Depot, staff will count and sort the containers based on the material they are made from.

Once sorted, containers are placed into large bags and sent to a plant for processing.

Processing

All used beverage containers in our province arrive at the processing plant in Mount Pearl, NL, where they are prepared for market. Employees use a large machine to compact the different types of containers into large cubes, called bales. Then, the bales of aluminum, steel, and different types of plastic are shipped to recycling plants.

Fun fact: One bale of aluminum may contain 17,000 aluminum cans – and weigh over 550 pounds!

Transformation into Something New

The bales are sent to various facilities across North America, where they’re transformed into new products—sometimes more beverage containers, or even something entirely different! It’s a vital step in giving materials a second life and reducing waste.

How Are Beverage Containers Recycled and What Do They Become?

Some materials are recycled into products that can be recycled again – the recycle cycle never stops! For example, used aluminum cans often become new cans. This is called “closed-loop recycling.” Other times, materials find new life as something not easily recycled – for example, plastic may be used in carpets. This is called “open-loop recycling.”

You might be surprised by the potential of a used beverage container collected in Newfoundland and Labrador, and how many different things it can become!

Plastic

All plastic beverage containers, including PET and HDPE, are purchased by Hebert’s Recycling Inc. Containers are ground into flakes/chips that are sold to various manufacturers in North America, who use them for the production of new products.

Possible uses for recycled plastic include:

  • New plastic containers and packaging
  • Insulation for jackets and sleeping bags
  • Fabric for hats, sweaters, jackets, or t-shirts
  • Fibre for carpets

Glass

Glass beverage containers (non-refillable) are sent to RAYAN in Moncton, New Brunswick. There, the glass is crushed into fine pieces called “cullet” and sold to markets throughout North America for production of new products.

Possible uses for recycled glass include:

  • New glass bottles
  • Fibreglass insulation
  • Reflective asphalt paint

Tetra and Gable

Tetra and gable top beverage containers (such as juice boxes) are purchased by Hebert’s Recycling Inc. These products have more materials than simply fibre – there are coatings and other components which make reclamation and recycling into a more involved process.

Containers are sent to paper mills that specialize in recovering the highest grade of fibre. The material is mixed with water to create a pulp mixture that allows paper fibre to be separated from the plastic layers and coatings. The paper fibre is then manufactured into boxboard and other paper products. Any other material like plastics are also recycled if they can be reclaimed.

Possible uses for recycled tetra and gable include:

  • Boxboard for products like cereal and crackers
  • Paper towels
  • Tissue
  • Corrugated cardboard

Aluminum Cans

Aluminum cans are sent to the Schupan facility in Kentucky – a renowned aluminum supplier with high sustainability standards. There, the aluminum is shredded, melted and rolled into sheets to be cut into new aluminum products, using innovative processes that maximize the yield of this final step on the recycling journey.

Fun fact: The aluminum can you recycle today can be back on the shelf in just 60 days – ready to use again!

Possible uses for recycled aluminum include:

  • Aluminum cans
  • Aluminum foil
  • Siding
  • Pie plates
  • Small appliances

Steel Cans

Almost all the original material from steel beverage containers is recovered during recycling. Steel containers are sent to companies like John Ross & Sons and/or other various brokers. From there, it is sold to markets throughout North America for production of new steel products.

Possible uses for recycled steel include:

  • Steel cans
  • Appliances
  • Automobiles
  • Construction materials

Giving Old Materials a New Life

Hebert’s Recycling Inc. is a New Brunswick-based, family-owned recycling company with a recycling centre location in Mount Pearl, Newfoundland and Labrador. They work with MMSB to collect, process, and transport recyclable materials from our province’s Green Depot locations.

Beverage Distributors

The Used Beverage Container Recycling Program is part of Newfoundland and Labrador’s Waste Management Regulations, which require that all beverage distributors in the province to register for the program and complete reports and remits.

What’s Accepted / Not Accepted

  • To help Green Depot operators and staff perform their jobs as safely and effectively as possible, it’s important they be able to clearly identify the containers they receive to ensure they are included in the Beverage Container Recycling Program, so labels should be left on wherever possible.

      Collapse

  • There are several reasons:

    • Green Depot operators and staff must be able to clearly identify what product container is being returned, and crushed or flattened containers can be difficult to identify.
    • To ensure the health and safety of Green Depot staff, there’s a maximum weight that each bulk bag can accommodate based on the number of containers. When containers are crushed or flattened, more of them can fit into bulk bags, putting them over the acceptable weight limits.
    • When containers are processed, they are compacted into large cubes called bales. Containers that have been crushed or flattened often won’t take the right shape and join with other containers in a bale, resulting in inadequate bales that could be rejected by our partners in the next step of the recycling journey or have to be disposed of in a landfill.

      Collapse

  • Milk, goat’s milk, and fortified plant-based milk beverages like soy, almond, and oat beverages, as defined under the Canadian Food and Drug Regulations, are deemed essential nutritional products, and as such, no recycling deposit is charged. For this reason, milk cartons are not accepted for refund at the Green Depot.

    The exception applies to milk alternatives that are labelled as “fortified plant-based beverages” and “not sources of protein.” These beverages are included in the Used Beverage Container Recycling Program and are accepted at Green Depot.

    Note: Milk containers are accepted in curb-side recycling, where programs are available.

    Learn More

      Collapse

  • Refillable beer bottles are not part of MMSB’s Used Beverage Container Recycling Program because local beer brewers such as Labatt, Molson, and Quidi Vidi operate an independent deposit-refund system. 

    As an added service for their customers, some Green Depot locations will accept refillable beer bottles. Please call your local depot to confirm whether refillable beer bottles are accepted as well as the amount of the refund. Find your Green Depot location here. 

    Refillable bottles can be returned to some beer retailers or to bottle depots to receive a refund.

    Note: ALL imported beer bottles and beer cans purchased in the province are accepted through MMSB’s program and can be returned to a Green Depot for refund.

  • The Green Depot network is comprised of a network of independently owned and operated businesses. Some locations offer services that are separate from the used beverage container licensing agreement with MMSB, meaning services offered will vary from location to location.

Schools and Non-Profits

  • MMSB is unable to expand the Get Matched! Program beyond K-12 schools as it is not financially feasible due to the high volume of community groups and charities in Newfoundland and Labrador. Our efforts to match recycling refunds for K-12 schools throughout the province are meant to encourage responsible waste habits among youth and to help schools raise money that can be reinvested in environmental initiatives.

      Collapse

  • Many non-profit groups collect beverage containers to raise funds. You can donate by giving your beverage containers directly to the group or by putting your beverage containers on that group’s Xpress account at the Green Depot.

      Collapse

  • Any K-12 public or private school in Newfoundland and Labrador can register with the Get Matched! School Recycling Program each year. Once a school is registered, MMSB will match the amount of the school’s recycling refund and provide the matching funds through a monthly payment to the school.

    You can help bolster a school’s refund by donating your recyclable beverage containers directly to the school – OR, when dropping off beverage containers at a Green Depot by informing a staff member that you want your containers to count for a particular school (this is important because account balances cannot later be transferred to a school account and will not qualify for the matching refund payment).
